Literature Mapping Task Objective 

Evaluate the Human-AI Research Continuity Theory (HARCT) framework against existing academic literature.

The goal is not to improve the theory internally.

The goal is to determine:

What is genuinely novel? What already exists? What overlaps with prior research? What contribution remains after removing overlaps? 

Assume the role of a literature reviewer preparing a publication assessment.

Evaluate the following theory documents.

Compare HARCT with Distributed Cognition.

Questions:

What similarities exist? What differences exist? Does HARCT explain anything Distributed Cognition does not? 

Provide assessment.

Task 2 

Compare HARCT with Extended Mind Theory.

Questions:

Is Externalized Memory simply an example of the Extended Mind? Does Repository as Cognitive Infrastructure add anything beyond Extended Mind concepts? Does HARCT contribute a new perspective? 

Provide assessment.

Task 3 

Compare HARCT with Organizational Memory research.

Questions:

Are memory repositories equivalent to organizational memory systems? Is Distributed Research Memory genuinely distinct? What is novel? 

Provide assessment.

Task 4 

Compare HARCT with Knowledge Management literature.

Questions:

Are README files, repositories, and handovers simply knowledge management artifacts? Does HARCT explain more than traditional knowledge management frameworks? 

Provide assessment.

Task 5 

Compare HARCT with Personal Knowledge Management (PKM).

Questions:

Is Future-Self Collaboration merely a form of note-taking? Is it equivalent to PKM systems such as Zettelkasten, Obsidian, or digital note systems? If not, what is different? 

Provide assessment.

Task 6 

Compare HARCT with Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and CSCW literature.

Questions:

Are Human-AI Working Agreements simply interaction patterns? Do collaboration protocols represent a novel contribution? What overlaps already exist? 

Provide assessment.

Task 7 

Compare HARCT with Research Infrastructure literature.

Questions:

Are repositories simply research infrastructure? Does Repository as Cognitive Infrastructure provide a meaningful extension? 

Provide assessment.

Task 8 

Identify Genuine Novelty.

After removing overlap with prior work, identify:

High Novelty 

Concepts that appear genuinely new.

Medium Novelty 

Concepts that combine existing ideas in a new way.

Low Novelty 

Concepts largely explained by existing literature.

Provide justification.

Task 9 

Identify Publication Contribution.

Questions:

If HARCT were submitted to an academic venue:

What would reviewers identify as the main contribution? What would reviewers identify as the weakest contribution? Which concepts are most publishable? 

Provide assessment.

Task 10 

Construct a Literature Positioning Map.

Produce a map similar to:

Distributed Cognition ↓ Extended Mind ↓ Knowledge Management ↓ HARCT

or any alternative structure that better reflects the literature.

The objective is to position HARCT within existing academic work.

Expected Output 

Produce:

Literature overlap map Prior-art analysis Novelty ranking Strongest contribution Weakest contribution Recommended literature to cite Recommended positioning strategy Publication-level assessment 

Focus on comparison with existing literature rather than internal theory development.

The objective is to determine whether HARCT represents a genuinely new contribution or a reorganization of existing concepts.

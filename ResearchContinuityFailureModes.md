ResearchContinuityFailureModes.md
Purpose
Human-AI Research Continuity Theory explains how long-term research can be sustained.
However, a complementary question remains.
Why does research continuity fail?
This document proposes a classification of failure modes observed during the Minor Thesis research program.
Understanding these failures helps explain why memory repositories, handover systems, collaboration protocols, and repository infrastructure became necessary.
1. Observation
Research interruptions were rarely caused by a lack of intelligence.
More commonly, interruptions emerged from failures of continuity.
Repeated disruptions included:
Forgotten context
Lost priorities
Repository confusion
Session termination
AI transitions
Token exhaustion
These failures often prevented progress despite the availability of information.
2. Human Forgetting Failure
Human memory is incomplete.
Examples include:
Forgotten discoveries
Forgotten decisions
Forgotten relationships among projects
As research duration increased, remembering every detail became impossible.
Without external support, continuity deteriorated.
3. AI Context Loss Failure
AI systems operate within limited context windows.
Consequently:
Earlier discussions become inaccessible.
Historical reasoning disappears.
Long-term context fragments.
This creates recurring reconstruction requirements.
4. Session Boundary Failure
Research frequently spans many sessions.
Each new session introduces risk.
Examples:
Repeated explanations
Repeated onboarding
Repeated context reconstruction
The cost of restarting accumulates over time.
5. Token Exhaustion Failure
Long-term collaboration repeatedly encounters token constraints.
Common effects include:
Premature conversation termination
Incomplete analysis
Forced summarization
Context compression
Token exhaustion therefore functions as a continuity threat rather than merely a technical limitation.
6. Repository Fragmentation Failure
As repositories multiply, relationships become harder to track.
Symptoms include:
Duplicate ideas
Isolated projects
Missing connections
Navigation difficulties
Without integration mechanisms, research structure becomes fragmented.
7. Handover Failure
Transitions between AI systems introduce risk.
Failures occur when:
Context is not documented.
Summaries are incomplete.
Project status is unclear.
Poor handovers increase reconstruction cost and reduce continuity.
8. Goal Drift Failure
One of the most important failures observed during the research program was Goal Drift.
Goal Drift occurs when:
Local tasks replace strategic objectives.
Short-term activity dominates long-term direction.
The original research purpose becomes unclear.
This failure may occur even when information is preserved successfully.
9. Future-Self Communication Failure
Future-Self Collaboration depends on artifact quality.
Failures occur when:
Documentation is incomplete.
Context is ambiguous.
Repository structures become unclear.
In these cases, future researchers must repeat previous work.
The communication channel across time weakens.
10. Reconstruction Failure
Research continuity ultimately depends on successful reconstruction.
Failure occurs when:
Context cannot be restored.
Relationships cannot be recovered.
Priorities cannot be identified.
At this point continuity collapses.
The project effectively behaves as if it were starting from the beginning.
11. Failure Hierarchy
The observed failures can be organized as:
Goal Drift ↓ Context Loss ↓ Reconstruction Failure ↓ Research Interruption
This hierarchy suggests that continuity failure is often a systemic process rather than a single event.
12. Failure Modes Hypothesis
The following hypothesis is proposed.
Long-term Human-AI research fails when reconstruction cost exceeds the ability of participants to restore research context and goals.
Under this hypothesis, continuity mechanisms are valuable because they reduce the probability of failure.
13. Relationship to Human-AI Research Continuity Theory
Failure Modes Theory explains the necessity of the major components of HARCT.
Human Forgetting ↓ Externalized Memory
AI Context Loss ↓ Context Transfer
AI Transition ↓ Handover Systems
Goal Drift ↓ Goal Preservation
Reconstruction Failure ↓ Repository Infrastructure
Under this interpretation, Human-AI Research Continuity Theory can be viewed as a collection of mechanisms designed to counter specific continuity failures.

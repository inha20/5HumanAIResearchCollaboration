Repository Purpose and Status Purpose of This Repository 

This repository documents a research program investigating long-term Human-AI collaboration.

The primary goal is not to evaluate AI intelligence.

The primary goal is to understand how research continuity can be maintained when humans and AI systems collaborate across long periods of time.

The repository examines:

Memory preservation Context transfer AI-to-AI handover Repository-centered research workflows Future-self collaboration Distributed research memory Research continuity Position Within the Research Program 

This repository is the fifth major repository in the Minor Thesis research program.

Research progression:

K-Map Structure Invariance Symmetric Boolean Function Minor Thesis Variable Rearrangement Invariance Minor Thesis Structure Recognition Theory Human-AI Research Collaboration 

The first four repositories focus primarily on mathematical structures and structure recognition.

This repository shifts attention toward the research process itself.

As a result, the research program becomes partially self-referential.

The process used to create the research program becomes an object of study.

Current Research Question 

The central research question has gradually evolved into:

How can research continuity be maintained when neither humans nor AI systems possess sufficient memory to preserve complete research context?

This question currently serves as the organizing problem for the repository.

Repository Philosophy 

This repository is based on a simple observation.

Long-term research rarely fails because participants are unable to generate ideas.

Long-term research more often fails because context, goals, and continuity are lost over time.

Accordingly, the repository treats continuity as a first-class research problem.

The central assumption is:

Sustainable Human-AI collaboration depends on preserving, restoring, and transferring research context across time.

Whether this assumption is correct remains an open research question.

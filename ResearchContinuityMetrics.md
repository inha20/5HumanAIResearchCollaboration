ResearchContinuityMetrics.md
Purpose
Human-AI Research Continuity Theory proposes that research continuity is a measurable phenomenon.
However, continuity itself is abstract.
This document proposes measurable indicators that may be used to evaluate research continuity.
1. The Measurement Problem
Most research evaluation focuses on:
Publications
Citations
Productivity
Output quantity
HARCT proposes a different focus.
The primary concern is not how much knowledge exists.
The primary concern is how effectively knowledge can be recovered and reused.
Therefore, continuity requires separate metrics.
2. Reconstruction Time
Definition:
The amount of time required to restore sufficient context to resume productive work.
Examples:
Returning after one week
Returning after one month
Returning after AI replacement
Lower reconstruction time indicates stronger continuity.
3. Reconstruction Cost
Definition:
Total effort required to restore context.
Possible components:
Reading time
Token consumption
Documentation review effort
Repository navigation effort
Lower reconstruction cost indicates stronger continuity.
4. Goal Recovery Rate
Definition:
The probability that the original research objective can be correctly recovered after interruption.
Examples:
Research goals remain identifiable.
Priorities remain recoverable.
Long-term direction remains visible.
Higher goal recovery rates indicate stronger continuity.
5. Handover Success Rate
Definition:
The probability that a new collaborator can successfully continue work after reviewing available artifacts.
Possible collaborators:
Another AI system
Another researcher
Future self
Higher success rates indicate better context transfer.
6. AI Replaceability Score
Definition:
The degree to which a project can continue after replacing an AI system.
Examples:
ChatGPT ↓ Claude
Claude ↓ Future AI
If continuity remains high, replaceability is high.
7. Repository Recoverability
Definition:
The ability to reconstruct project context from repository contents alone.
Indicators:
README quality
Documentation completeness
Repository organization
Higher recoverability implies stronger infrastructure.
8. Future-Self Recovery Time
Definition:
The time required for a future researcher to recover sufficient context to continue work.
Observed workflow:
Future Self ↓ Repository Review ↓ Context Reconstruction ↓ Research Continuation
Lower recovery time indicates stronger future-self collaboration.
9. Continuity Score
A conceptual continuity score may combine:
Reconstruction Time
Reconstruction Cost
Goal Recovery Rate
Handover Success Rate
AI Replaceability
Repository Recoverability
Higher scores indicate stronger research continuity.
10. Predicted Relationships
HARCT predicts:
Better Documentation ↓ Lower Reconstruction Cost
Lower Reconstruction Cost ↓ Higher Continuity
Higher Continuity ↓ Longer Sustainable Research Programs
11. Research Continuity Measurement Hypothesis
The following hypothesis is proposed.
Research continuity can be evaluated through measurable indicators related to context recovery, goal preservation, and reconstruction cost.
Under this hypothesis, continuity becomes a research variable rather than a purely conceptual idea.

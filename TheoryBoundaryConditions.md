TheoryBoundaryConditions.md
Purpose
Every theory has limits.
A theory becomes stronger when it clearly identifies the situations it can explain and the situations it cannot explain.
This document proposes boundary conditions for Human-AI Research Continuity Theory (HARCT).
The objective is to define the scope of the theory and identify situations where its explanatory power may decrease.
1. The Boundary Problem
HARCT was developed from observations collected during the Minor Thesis research program.
The research environment possessed several unusual characteristics:
Long duration
Multiple AI systems
Multiple repositories
Repeated context reconstruction
Explicit documentation practices
Therefore, an important question emerges:
Under what conditions should HARCT be expected to apply?
2. Short-Term Projects
Situation
Projects lasting:
Minutes
Hours
Days
with little interruption.
Assessment
Research continuity is rarely a dominant challenge.
Context remains active.
Reconstruction requirements remain low.
Prediction
HARCT should have limited explanatory value.
Traditional productivity models may be more useful.
3. Long-Term Projects
Situation
Projects lasting:
Months
Years
with repeated interruptions.
Assessment
Context loss becomes common.
Goal preservation becomes important.
Reconstruction cost increases.
Prediction
HARCT should provide strong explanatory power.
4. Single-AI Environments
Situation
Only one AI system participates.
Assessment
AI-to-AI handover becomes irrelevant.
However:
Context loss
Reconstruction
Goal preservation
remain important.
Prediction
HARCT remains partially applicable.
5. Multi-AI Environments
Situation
Multiple AI systems participate.
Examples:
ChatGPT
Claude
ANTIGRAVITY
Future systems
Assessment
Context transfer becomes critical.
AI replaceability becomes important.
Prediction
HARCT becomes more valuable.
6. Human-Only Research
Situation
No AI systems participate.
Assessment
Several concepts remain applicable:
Externalized Memory
Goal Preservation
Future-Self Collaboration
Reconstruction Cost
However:
AI-to-AI Handover
Human-AI Working Agreements
disappear.
Prediction
Some components generalize beyond AI collaboration.
7. AI-Only Systems
Situation
Research is performed entirely by AI systems.
Assessment
Current observations provide insufficient evidence.
Questions remain:
Can goals persist?
Can continuity survive model replacement?
Can AI systems preserve research identity?
Prediction
Additional study is required.
8. Repository-Free Environments
Situation
No repositories exist.
Assessment
HARCT predicts:
Higher reconstruction cost
Reduced continuity
Increased context loss
Alternative memory systems would be required.
9. Goal-Free Environments
Situation
Goals change continuously.
Assessment
Goal Preservation Theory predicts:
Weak continuity
Fragmented projects
Reduced program emergence
Research continuity becomes difficult.
10. Extreme Automation
Situation
Most activities become automated.
Assessment
The theory predicts that continuity challenges remain.
Automation may reduce labor.
It does not eliminate:
Context transfer
Goal preservation
Reconstruction
11. Boundary Conditions Summary
HARCT is expected to be strongest when:
Research duration is long.
Multiple interruptions occur.
Multiple systems participate.
Reconstruction is necessary.
Goals remain stable.
HARCT is expected to be weakest when:
Projects are short.
Context remains continuously available.
Reconstruction is unnecessary.
12. Boundary Condition Hypothesis
The following hypothesis is proposed.
The explanatory power of Human-AI Research Continuity Theory increases as research duration, interruption frequency, and context reconstruction requirements increase.
Under this hypothesis, continuity becomes increasingly important as projects become larger and more distributed.
13. Relationship to HARCT
Boundary Conditions Theory defines where HARCT should and should not be expected to work.
This improves theoretical precision and reduces the risk of overgeneralization.
A theory that explains everything often explains nothing.
A theory that clearly identifies its boundaries becomes more scientifically useful.

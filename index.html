Abstract

Executive Abstract

Long-term Human-AI collaboration presents challenges that extend beyond the capabilities of either human memory or AI memory alone. This study investigates long-term Human-AI research collaboration through a multi-repository research program and proposes Human-AI Research Continuity Theory (HARCT) as a unified explanatory framework: long-term Human-AI research collaboration is fundamentally a problem of research continuity rather than intelligence alone.

HARCT is supported by four theoretical components: Goal Preservation Theory (preserving goals enables context reconstruction), Cognitive Architecture Theory (a distributed system of human goal memory, AI reasoning, and artifact storage), Reconstruction Cost Theory (documentation quality as an economic variable determining efficiency), and Externalized Memory (storing context outside participants through persistent artifacts).

The findings suggest that sustainable long-term Human-AI research depends not on perfect memory, but on constructing systems that preserve goals, minimize reconstruction costs, and transfer context across time, sessions, and participants.

Central Claim — HARCT

"Long-term Human-AI research collaboration is fundamentally a problem of
research continuity rather than intelligence alone."

Current AI discourse overwhelmingly emphasizes model intelligence, parameter scale, or context window size. HARCT argues that in scientific inquiry across multi-month horizons, the bottleneck is not intelligence per se, but the ability to preserve, restore, and transfer research context across session boundaries, model upgrades, and repository branches.

Four Theoretical Components

Component 1 · High Novelty Goal Preservation Theory Preserving research goals is more fundamental than preserving information. Goals enable context reconstruction; information without direction becomes an unusable archive. When goals survive interruptions, context can be rebuilt.
Component 2 · Core Architecture Cognitive Architecture Theory Research continuity requires three interacting components: (1) Human long-term goal memory, (2) AI short-term reasoning capability, and (3) Artifact-based context storage. None is sufficient alone.
Component 3 · Economic Modeling Reconstruction Cost Theory Documentation quality is an explicit economic variable determining collaboration efficiency. As research duration increases, reconstruction cost is hypothesized to become a dominant factor. Poor documentation is expensive.
Component 4 · Infrastructure Externalized Memory Context is preserved outside active participants through repositories, handover files, and reminder systems. The critical property is recoverability rather than passive storage capacity.

How the Components Interact

1. Goal Preservation Determines what must be preserved — research direction and strategic identity
↓
2. Externalized Memory Provides where context is stored — in structured artifacts outside human and AI memory
↓
3. Reconstruction Cost Determines why documentation quality matters — as an economic efficiency variable
↓
4. Cognitive Architecture Explains how labor is distributed — human provides goals, AI reconstructs, artifacts store
↓
Research Continuity Emerges sustainably when all four components operate in unison

Key Concepts & Terminology

Future-Self Collaboration

Treating documentation as communication with a temporally displaced self who will lack the present train of thought.

Memory Repository

Any artifact capable of preserving research context and supporting future context restoration; the critical property is recoverability, not storage alone.

AI-to-AI Handover

Context transfer protocols that allow switching from one AI model/vendor to another with minimal loss of momentum.

Distributed Research Memory

A cognitive system where no single participant possesses the entire state; continuity emerges from the network.

Reconstruction Cost ($RCost$)

The time, token, and cognitive expenditure required to re-establish a productive working state after an interruption.

Context Transfer Quality ($CT$)

The high-fidelity transmission of implicit assumptions, architectural invariants, and next steps across time.

Testable Predictions & Candidate Formulation

RC ∝ (GP × CT) / RCost

Research Continuity ($RC$) is proportional to Goal Preservation ($GP$) and Context Transfer ($CT$), inversely proportional to Reconstruction Cost ($RCost$).

P3 Programs with structured handover artifacts recover context with significantly lower token and time overhead.
P5 Explicit goal preservation enables projects to survive multi-month dormancy and resume without mission drift.
P6 As research lifespan increases from weeks to years, reconstruction cost becomes the dominant predictor of project survival.
P7 Attaching knowledge to external artifacts rather than AI conversational state makes AI models more readily replaceable.
